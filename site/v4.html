Robot Lab v4: every joint gets a real mechanism

Bryan looked at the v3 model and said some joints couldn't work in the real world. He was right: v3 was a simplified parametric model that had fallen behind the engineered design. v4 rebuilds the legs from the module parts: the Actuators STEP solids (hip yaw/roll housings, twin RV12 roller-screw bodies, hip lever with needle hub, knee lever r47.5), the Legs linkage geometry, the ankle instep pins at r73, the ±18° ankle-roll stop, the femur tongue with the IKO TLA3520 needle pivot and the bonded thigh clamp. Every joint now shows its bearing, its actuator attachment points and its hard stop. Upper-body joints are strain-wave + crossed-roller placeholders (orange), since no module has engineered them yet. Their dimensions are estimates.

Update 14 Oct (Actuators D59-D62): the knee now uses one twin housing with a shared centre web. The ankle screws now have an axial GE 12 C top eye in a shank clevis and a QA1 MGF12T-class PTFE rod end at the bottom (27°). The hip-body clearance is now measured, not typed in. Legs L37-L40 (15c602d) add the matching leg side: ankle clevises at trunnion z 450, the instep lug moved 5 mm outboard on an outrigger, and hip yaw/roll PU bumper stops (yaw -35/+45, roll -12/+15). Still estimated: the trunnion bracket plates and the hip yoke brackets. Ankle pitch -60° clashes, but that is outside the ±45/+40 range used in simulation.

6 buildable · 26 placeholder · 0 fail (joints per side; 15 finger joints per hand). Standing pose: 0 interferences, 0 floating parts.
